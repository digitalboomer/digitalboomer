# 1. Logo

Our logo shows **two older people laughing together at a laptop** above the lettering “Digital Boomer”.

**The two people at the laptop symbolize:**

- Learning together: support at eye level, nobody has to learn alone

- Self-confident older people who are curious and capable of learning – never helpless

**Thumbs up, waving and the little sparks symbolize:**

- Joy, success and “I did it!” moments in everyday digital life

- Staying connected with family and friends

**The turquoise swoosh symbolizes:**

- Movement and connection – a friendly gesture that underlines the name

Together they represent our mission: to explain the digital world step by step – patiently, understandably and with a smile.

**Rules for using the logo**

- **Clear space:** keep at least the height of the letter “D” free on all sides

- **Minimum size:** main logo at least 180 px wide (print: 45 mm), wordmark at least 120 px (30 mm), emblem at least 64 px

- **Do not alter:** do not stretch, rotate, recolor or add shadows or effects; do not retype the lettering

- **Background:** use the logo on white or on the warm off-white background (#F7F8F5); on dark or colored areas, place it on a white panel

- **Relation to the UI colors:** the turquoise in the logo is artwork and is not used for text. The accessible UI counterpart is the deep teal \#176B68 (section 4)

# 2. Name

**Digital** stands for:

- Today’s digital world: smartphone, tablet, messenger, online banking and AI tools

- Digital participation: everyone should be able to take part in everyday life independently and safely

**Boomer** stands for:

- A clear, direct address to the generation that grew up without smartphones and brings a lot of life experience

- A friendly wink – meant respectfully and appreciatively, never patronizing

Digital Boomer is more than a name – it is a promise: we guide people step by step into the digital world and give them the confidence to try new things.

**Lettering**

- The lettering is part of the logo artwork and is not retyped in another font

- “Digital” in dark blue, “Boomer” in turquoise – both words on one line, bold, rounded sans-serif letters

**Language and tone of voice**

- **Language:** this style guide and all UI examples are in English. The website itself is written in German and addresses users formally (German “Sie”)

- **Respectful:** friendly and patient, no technical jargon; address users politely and formally (in German: “Sie”) and avoid unnecessary foreign-language terms

- **Simple:** short sentences, one idea per sentence; always explain technical terms (e.g. “App – a program on your smartphone”)

- **Encouraging:** never make users feel inexperienced, incapable or embarrassed; mistakes are normal and no problem

- **Concrete:** name the action (“Tap the green symbol”) instead of abstract descriptions

**Reassuring key phrases**

|--------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| “Step by step into everyday digital life.” | “You can do it – at your own pace.”                      |
| “No prior knowledge required.”             | “Just getting started? Then you are in the right place.” |

# 3. Design Principles for Older Users

Our target group is older adults with little experience of digital media – capable, curious learners who sometimes feel uncertain about technology. With age, eyesight, fine motor skills and reaction speed often change. The design must never look childish or outdated and never stereotype its users. All decisions in this style guide therefore follow these principles and the requirements of **WCAG 2.2 Level AA**:

| **Principle**               | **Implementation**                                                                                                                                                              |
|-----------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Large, readable text**    | Body text 18–20 px (mobile 18 px); clear heading hierarchy; line spacing 1.6; all text resizable up to 200 % without loss of content; no thin or decorative fonts               |
| **Strong contrast**         | Normal text at least 4.5 : 1, large text and UI component boundaries at least 3 : 1 (WCAG AA). Core text colors reach 12 : 1 or more; no light gray text                        |
| **Large click areas**       | Interactive elements at least 44 × 44 px (buttons 48–56 px high), at least 8 px between neighbouring targets                                                                    |
| **Clear navigation**        | Five main items, always visible and labeled: Home, Topics, All tutorials, Digital safety, About us. Logo = home link; breadcrumbs and a visible “Back” button on every sub page |
| **Icons with text**         | An icon never stands alone – it is always accompanied by a word or has a descriptive accessible label                                                                           |
| **Visible keyboard focus**  | Every interactive element shows a clear 3 px focus ring with a 2 px gap; no function depends on mouse hover                                                                     |
| **Forms that help**         | Visible labels above every field (no placeholder-only labels); friendly, specific error messages with a hint on how to fix them                                                 |
| **Never color alone**       | Always combine color, icon and text (e.g. success = teal + check mark + “Well done!”). Red is reserved for genuine errors and security warnings                                 |
| **Calm and predictable**    | Little content per page, lots of white space; no autoplay, no flashing content, no time limits; motion only when needed and switched off for “reduce motion”                    |
| **Understandable videos**   | Short (2–5 minutes), slow pace, captions and a transcript, pause and “Watch again”; a step-by-step summary below the video                                                      |
| **Accessible alternatives** | Every screenshot or illustration has a text alternative and the steps are also written out in numbered text                                                                     |
| **Consistent everywhere**   | Same elements in the same place on every page; reusable components instead of page-by-page design                                                                               |

# 5. Typography

## 5.1 Typeface: Atkinson Hyperlegible

- Developed by the Braille Institute for people with low vision

- Every letter is clearly distinguishable – even similar characters such as I, l, 1 or O, 0

- Very readable in long texts and on small screens; Regular (400) for body text, Bold (700) for headings, buttons and emphasis

- Free to use via Google Fonts (SIL Open Font License)

- One family for headings and body keeps the page calm and consistent. No thin, light or decorative weights

- Fallback fonts (if the web font does not load): Source Sans 3, Verdana, Arial, sans-serif





## 5.2 Hierarchy on the Website

- Headings are set in Bold, in navy; body text in Regular, in dark slate

- Size and weight create the hierarchy – not color, italics or capital letters

- The page title (H1) appears exactly once per page; headings are never skipped (H1 → H2 → H3)



## 5.3 Font Sizes

The sizes are deliberately larger than on typical websites. Sizes are defined in rem (base 16 px = 1 rem) so that browser text enlargement works without breaking the layout.

| **Element**                   | **Desktop / tablet** |          | **Mobile** |           |
|-------------------------------|----------------------|----------|------------|-----------|
| **Body text**                 | 20 px                | 1.25 rem | 18 px      | 1.125 rem |
| **H1**                        | 48 px                | 3 rem    | 36 px      | 2.25 rem  |
| **H2**                        | 36 px                | 2.25 rem | 30 px      | 1.875 rem |
| **H3**                        | 28 px                | 1.75 rem | 24 px      | 1.5 rem   |
| **H4**                        | 24 px                | 1.5 rem  | 22 px      | 1.375 rem |
| **Button text**               | 20 px                | 1.25 rem | 20 px      | 1.25 rem  |
| **Caption / label (minimum)** | 16 px                | 1 rem    | 16 px      | 1 rem     |

**More rules for text**

- Line spacing: body text 1.6, headings 1.25; paragraph spacing at least 1 line

- Line length: at most about 65 characters per line; always left-aligned, never justified

- Emphasis: use bold – not italics, capital letters or underlining (underlining is reserved for links)

- Short paragraphs (maximum 3–4 lines), lists and numbered steps instead of long blocks of text

- Text can be enlarged to 200 % and zoomed to 400 % without cut-off content or horizontal scrolling

# 6. Graphics and Icons

## 6.1 Icon Style

- **Solid and simple:** filled symbols without fine detail that remain recognizable even with poor eyesight

- **Always labeled:** no icon stands alone – the word below or beside it is mandatory, or it carries a descriptive accessible name

- **Size:** at least 28 px, in the navigation 24–40 px; click area at least 44 × 44 px

- **Color:** navy \#17324D by default; teal \#176B68 for learning and security topics; red only for errors and security warnings; white on filled buttons

- **Familiar symbols:** magnifier, house, question mark – no abstract inventions

## 6.3 Imagery

- **Illustrations:** friendly, flat illustrations in the style of our logo – self-confident older people, rounded shapes, colors from our palette; meaningful (they explain a step), not merely decorative

- **Photos:** real, friendly people aged 55 and older in everyday situations; bright, natural light; no stock-photo clichés

- **Screenshots and mockups:** large and cropped to the essentials. The important spot is framed in navy (3 px, white outline) and marked with a numbered navy circle; the numbers are explained in text below the image

- **Alternative text:** every instructional image has a text alternative, and the same information is also given in the numbered steps

- **Avoid:** cluttered collages, small details and text inside images. Real banking data is never shown – only fictional accounts and sample data



Example of the annotation style: a fictional phishing email with numbered markers and a plain-language explanation of each warning sign.

# 7. UI Elements (Design System)

General rules: corner radius 12 px (cards 16 px), spacing on an 8 px grid, interactive elements at least 44 × 44 px (buttons 48–56 px high). Every interactive element has visible default, hover, pressed and keyboard-focus states. Reusable components are used on every page instead of page-specific designs.

## 7.1 Spacing Scale

| **Token**   | **Value** | **Typical use**                                |
|-------------|-----------|------------------------------------------------|
| **space-1** | 4 px      | Gap between icon and label                     |
| **space-2** | 8 px      | Minimum gap between neighbouring click targets |
| **space-3** | 16 px     | Padding inside fields and small cards          |
| **space-4** | 24 px     | Padding of cards, gap between cards            |
| **space-5** | 32 px     | Space between content blocks                   |
| **space-6** | 48 px     | Space between page sections                    |
| **space-7** | 64 px     | Top and bottom space of hero areas             |

## 7.2 Buttons



- **Primary (navy):** the one main action per page, e.g. “Log in”, “Discover tutorials”, “Next step”

- **Secondary (teal):** learning actions, e.g. “Start tutorial”, “Keep learning”

- **Outline and text link:** “Back”, “How it works”; links are always underlined

- **States:** hover = darker color; pressed = darkest color; focus = 3 px navy ring with 2 px gap; success and error states always combine icon, text and color

## 7.3 Form Fields



- Visible label above every field; required fields are marked with the word “Required”, not only with an asterisk

- Password fields offer a “Show / Hide” button; password requirements are listed before typing

- Validation messages appear next to the field, are linked to it for screen readers and say what to do: “Please enter a valid email address, e.g. name@example.com.”

- Consent controls (privacy) are unticked by default and explained in plain language

## 7.4 Navigation



- Same header on every page; the logo always leads to the homepage; the current page is marked by bold text and a 4 px underline (not by color alone)

- On mobile the five items open in a full-width menu with large rows (at least 48 px)

- Breadcrumbs and a “Back” button allow a way back from every sub page; a “Home” link is available from every major section

## 7.5 Topic Cards and Tutorial Cards



- Topic card: icon, title, short description, number of tutorials, button “Open topic”

- Tutorial card: level, duration, icon, title, short explanation, button “Start tutorial” and a save toggle “Save / Saved”

- The whole card is clickable via its button only – one clear target per action; cards are stacked vertically on mobile

## 7.6 Progress Indicators and Learning Levels



- Progress always shows text (“Step 3 of 6”, “50 % done”) in addition to the bar

- Each learning level has a fixed icon, dot count and word – the level is clear even without color perception

## 7.7 Messages, Warnings and Quiz Feedback



- **Success** (teal), **information** (pale blue), **security warning and error** (red) – each with its own icon, a bold title and one plain sentence

- Security warnings are highlighted clearly but calmly; they tell users what to do instead of causing fear

- Quiz feedback names the result (“Correct”, “Not quite”) and always explains why – never blames the user

## 7.8 Dialogs

- Dialogs are only opened by a user action – never automatically or as a pop-up

- Dialog has a visible title, a clear close button “Close” and can be closed with the Esc key

- Keyboard focus moves into the dialog, stays inside while it is open and returns to the triggering button afterwards

- Used for confirmations (e.g. “Complete tutorial?”) – not for important content

# 9. Accessibility Validation

This style guide defines design decisions that support WCAG 2.2 Level AA. It does **not** claim that the finished website is fully WCAG-conformant. Conformance can only be confirmed by testing the real implementation.

**Checked in this guide**

- Contrast ratios of all palette combinations (calculated, section 4.5)

- Minimum sizes of text and click areas defined (sections 5 and 7)

- Meaning is never conveyed by color alone: icon, text or shape accompanies every state

**Still to be tested manually on the real website**

- Keyboard operation of every page and component, logical focus order, visible focus on all elements

- Screen reader tests (e.g. NVDA, VoiceOver): headings, landmarks, form labels, error messages, dialogs, quiz feedback

- Zoom to 200 % / 400 % and text enlargement; reflow at 320 px width on phone, tablet and desktop

- Real rendering of colors in the browser, including dark/high-contrast modes and the actual font loading

- Captions, transcripts and text alternatives: correctness and completeness

- Automated checks (e.g. axe, WAVE, Lighthouse) followed by a test with real users from the target group

- Legal pages (privacy, accessibility statement, imprint) are placeholders and need legal review before a real launch
